Revision 1.1 (March 1, 2023)

Since the first edition on June 1, 2022, I have corrected a theorem statement and improved several proofs. Regarding exercises, four are improved, three more have solutions and ten new ones are added. I have reorganized the treatment of the variations of the Banach Contraction Theorem working towards a more parallel treatment of discrete and continuous time problems.

For their extensive help in helping me understand this material, I wish to thank Veronica Centorrino, Alexander Davydov, Giulia De Pasquale, Robin Delabays, Anand Gokhale, Giovanni Russo, Kevin D. Smith, and M. Elena Valcher.

Detailed list of changes: In Chapter 1, added Remark 1.2 “Continuity and related concepts”, reorganized Section 1.4 “Variations on the theme by Banach”, added Figure 1.1, expanded E1.1, added solution to E1.6, added E1.7 and E1.8. In Chapter 2, improved the proof of Lemma 2.1, improved Figure 2.3, added E2.4, improved E2.9, E2.14 and E2.26, added E2.28, E2.29, E2.30 and E2.31. In Chapter 3, added Remark 3.6 “Existence and uniqueness theorems for ordinary differential equations” and clarified smoothness assumptions on vector fields throughout the chapter, added E3.4, improved E3.5, added solutions to E3.12, added E3.15 and E3.16. In Chapter 4, removed an incorrect claim in Theorem 4.4, added solution to E4.4. In Chapter 5, corrected the orthonormal basis in the example in Section 5.2.

March 1, 2023, Santa Barbara, California, USA. Francesco Bullo

Revision 1.2 (August 1, 2024)

In this revision, Chapter 2 has been heavily edited and much work has focused on exercises. In this edition, recommended exercises for readers and instructors are marked with the symbol ▶.

For help with the changes in this revision, I wish to thank Andrew Alleyne, Veronica Centorrino, Lily Cothren, Emiliano Dall'Anese, Alexander Davydov, Robin Delabays, Anand Gokhale, Zahra Marvi, Anton Proskurnikov, and Giovanni Russo.

Detailed list of changes: Chapter 1 contains an improved and simplified the treatment of local contraction and invariance in Lemma 1.10. Additionally, Exercise E1.3 on Lipschitz properties has been extended and the following new exercises added: E1.2 on the Newton–Raphson method and E1.5 on noisy contractions.

Chapter 2 has undergone significant reorganization and partial rewriting to enhance readability, consistency, and comprehensiveness. An appendix has been added on properties and transcriptions of LMIs. A running matrix example has been added, and the definition of weak pairings has been simplified, establishing their continuity properties via Exercise E2.26. The exercises have been renumbered to reflect the revised order of presentation. New exercises include E2.16 on the log norm of saddle matrices, E2.18 on Coppel's inequality with time-varying weights, and E2.29 on polyhedral norms and weak pairings. Exercise E2.35 on the ℓ2 logarithmic norm of block matrices with skew-symmetric off-diagonal properties has been extended and sharpened.

In Chapter 3, Section 3.7 has been revised to feature a more detailed analysis and literature review on contracting Lur'e systems, along with the new appendix 2.9.3 and the new exercises E3.21, E3.22, and E3.23. Exercise E3.13 on local infinitesimal contractivity and invariance has been improved. Additional exercises have been added: E3.9 on upper and lower bounds on discrete and continuous-time Lyapunov functions, E3.15 on contracting delay systems, E3.16 on contraction factors with respect to distinct norms, E3.18 on Lyapunov function for the variational system, and E3.20 on interconnected continuous and discrete-time systems.

In Chapter 4, Section 4.6 with a biochemical reaction network has been added. Exercise E4.8 has been improved. New exercises include E4.9 on the weighted Frobenius inner product, E4.2 on integral function as a third Lyapunov function, E4.11 on linear-exponential decay, and E4.12 on the convergence of weakly contracting and locally strongly contracting dynamics.

August 1, 2024, Santa Barbara, California, USA. Francesco Bullo

Revision 1.3 (January 1, 2026)

A new Chapter 6 has been added on stochastic dynamics. This chapter provides an overview of key concepts and equations related to the contraction analysis of stochastic differential equations and of the Fokker–Planck equation with respect to the Wasserstein metric.

Several typos have been corrected throughout. Substantial improvements have been made on the following Exercises: E1.1 on examples of complete metric spaces, E1.4 on the incremental small gain theorem, E1.8 on the Thomson metric for positive definite matrices, E1.9 on dynamic programming, E2.16 on the log norm of rank-1 matrices, E2.23 on least upper bounds on linear system solutions, E2.22 on the Grönwall comparison lemma with Dini derivatives, and E3.18 on the variational system and the sensitivity matrix.

The following new exercises have been added: E2.2 on Schur stability of 2 × 2 matrices, E2.6 on the vector space and norm properties of the function space Lp, E2.27 on interpolation properties of induced and logarithmic norms, E2.32 on matrix norm via weak pairings, E2.43 on nonEuclidean norms of interval matrices, E3.21 on Euclidean contractivity of symmetric neural networks, E3.22 on discrete-time firing-rate models, E3.23 on 1-Lipschitz layers in neural networks, E3.25 on incremental multipliers for elementwise maps, E3.29 on incremental multipliers and Jacobian properties, E3.30 on weighted gradient flows, E3.31 on contractivity of nonlinear systems in implicit Lur'e form, and E3.33 on a contracting gradient flow in the space of positive definite matrices.

For help with the changes in this revision, I wish to thank Lauren E. Conger, Alexander Davydov, Giancarlo Ferrari Trecate, Anand Gokhale, Yu Kawano, Moses Nah, Zahra Marvi, Anton Proskurnikov, and Ryotaro Shima.

January 1, 2026, Santa Barbara, California, USA. Francesco Bullo
